# Image prompts — WeThink

Written for ChatGPT's image generation. Paste one prompt per image.

Each prompt is built to defeat the specific things that make generated
photography look generated: glassy skin, impossible light, heavy background
blur, symmetrical compositions, and rooms with nothing out of place. The
technical camera language is doing real work here — asking for f/8 and a 35mm
lens is what keeps the whole room sharp instead of dissolving the background
into the creamy blur that reads instantly as fake.

Two practical notes before you start. Generated text on screens, whiteboards
and signage almost always comes out as garbled letterforms, which is the first
thing anyone notices, so every prompt below asks for shapes rather than words.
And whatever you generate, use it for atmosphere rather than captioning it as
your office or your team, because that is the line between styling a page and
making a claim.

---

## House style block

Prepend or append this to any prompt to keep the set consistent.

> Documentary editorial photograph, shot on a Fujifilm X-T5 with a 35mm lens at
> f/8, ISO 400, natural available light from windows mixed with warm interior
> lighting. Everything in the frame is in sharp focus from foreground to
> background — no shallow depth of field, no background blur, no bokeh. Colour
> is neutral and true, not graded or teal-and-orange. Slight sensor grain.
> Candid and unposed, caught mid-action rather than looking at the camera.
> Ordinary imperfection is welcome: cables, used coffee cups, a jacket over a
> chair, papers that are not squared up. No text, words, letters, logos or
> signage anywhere in the image — screens and whiteboards show only diagrams,
> charts, wireframe boxes and coloured shapes.

## 1. The office, mid-morning

> Documentary editorial photograph of a working technology consultancy office
> in Abu Dhabi on an ordinary weekday morning. An open room with six or seven
> desks, monitors showing wireframe layouts and coloured chart shapes, a
> whiteboard on the side wall covered in boxes and arrows drawn in marker.
> Four colleagues at work: an Emirati woman in a dark abaya and hijab at a desk
> with two monitors, a South Asian man in a light blue shirt standing while
> reviewing something on a laptop, an Arab man in his thirties in a plain
> polo shirt at the next desk, and a Filipina woman in smart casual clothes
> walking through the background carrying a notebook. Daylight comes through
> large windows on the left; the view outside is bright and slightly blown out
> so no skyline is distinguishable. Shot on a 35mm lens at f/8, everything in
> the frame in sharp focus, no background blur. Natural colour, mild grain,
> nobody looking at the camera. No text, letters, logos or signage anywhere.

## 2. A client meeting

> Documentary editorial photograph of a client meeting in a small glass-walled
> meeting room in an Abu Dhabi office. Five people around a light wood table:
> an Emirati man in a white kandura and ghutra listening with his hands folded,
> an Emirati woman in a dark abaya taking notes on a tablet, a middle-aged
> Levantine Arab woman in a blazer speaking and gesturing towards a wall screen
> that shows a simple bar chart and boxes, an Indian man in a grey shirt
> leaning forward, and a European man in his forties sitting back with a pen.
> Water glasses, a plate of dates, a laptop half-closed, papers spread
> unevenly. Overhead light mixed with daylight from the corridor. Shot on a
> 35mm lens at f/8 from the corner of the room, whole room sharp, no depth of
> field blur. Candid mid-conversation, nobody posed or looking at the camera.
> Natural colour, slight grain. No text, words, letters or logos anywhere.

## 3. A workshop

> Documentary editorial photograph of a hands-on technology workshop in a
> training room in Abu Dhabi. Twelve to fifteen participants seated at long
> tables with open laptops, turned towards a facilitator standing at the front
> beside a large screen showing a simple flow diagram of connected boxes. The
> facilitator is a woman in her thirties in a blazer, mid-sentence, one hand
> raised. The audience is mixed as Abu Dhabi actually is: Emirati men in
> kanduras, Emirati women in abayas, South Asian, Arab, Filipino and European
> professionals in business casual clothes, several taking notes by hand.
> Sticky notes in yellow and pink stuck to a side wall in an untidy cluster.
> Bright even room lighting, daylight from the back windows. Shot on a 35mm
> lens at f/8 from the back left of the room, every row in sharp focus, no
> background blur. Candid, natural colour, mild grain. No text, letters,
> numbers or logos anywhere in the frame.

## 4. An event

> Documentary editorial photograph of a technology industry event in a modern
> Abu Dhabi venue. Foreground: three professionals standing in conversation
> holding coffee cups, an Emirati man in a white kandura, a woman in a navy
> abaya and hijab, and a South Asian man in a suit with no tie, all mid-
> conversation and gesturing. Behind them a busy room with thirty or so
> attendees standing in small groups, a low stage on the right with an empty
> lectern, exhibition panels along the back wall showing abstract coloured
> shapes and diagrams. High ceilings, warm downlighting mixed with daylight
> from a glass wall. Shot on a 35mm lens at f/8 at chest height, the whole
> depth of the room in sharp focus, no blur on the crowd behind. Candid,
> nobody looking at the camera, natural colour, slight grain. No text, words,
> banners, letters or logos anywhere in the image.

## Variants worth generating

Run each prompt three or four times and keep the least perfect result. The
most convincing frame is usually the one with a slightly awkward composition
or a half-turned head, not the tidiest one.

Useful variations to append to any of the above:

- `Shot from a low angle, slightly off-centre composition.`
- `Late afternoon, warm low sun through the windows casting long shadows across the floor.`
- `Vertical 4:5 crop for social.` / `Wide 16:9 crop for a website banner.`
- `A quieter moment: only two people in the room, one at a desk, one standing.`

## What to check before using an image

Hands, always — count the fingers. Eyes and teeth, which tend to come out too
even. Any text that slipped in despite the instruction. Chair legs, cables and
window frames, where geometry goes wrong. Repeated faces in a crowd. Kandura
and abaya draping, which generators often get subtly wrong.

If a frame passes all of that and still looks a little ordinary, it is
probably the right one.
